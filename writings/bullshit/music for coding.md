Classic music is perfect for programming: the traditional harmonising and accuracy in tandem with natural sounds attune the brain for highly structured but creative work.

Noises are great for programming, white, brown, pink - occupy the cortext with constant signal for diffuse stimulus, keeping it engaged just enough and numb just enough for entering a flow state.

Nightcore is just great for coding: high pitched pitter patter sounds just enough like speech to occupy speech cortex, but not enough like normal speach to distract you, freeing space for actual work.

Trance and house music are great for software work - monotone rhythm creates a constant stream of "tacts" for your CPU to compute the best codes, and sweeping pads and seldom arpegios provide enough interrupt to not go into infinite cycle.

Heavy and industrial metal are insane for programming: screeching of guitars has a special effect on the brain, keeping it highly alert, but not irritated like from scratching glass thanks to being melodic.

Pop music is a very good help for programming, creating a familiar and safe atmosphere where the brain can relax and let go, focusing on the task at hand.

Throat singing is ingenious for programming: it sounds just human enough and alien enough to hit the sweet spon of attention diffusion, unlocking extra resources from the audio cortex.

Silence is hyper cool for coding - by having no input at all, an entire brain power becomes available in the time of need.
